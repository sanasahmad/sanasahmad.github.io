S Anas Ahmad

Researcher in English & Digital Humanities

I work at the intersection of postcolonial studies, digital humanities, game studies, and electronic literature, examining how cultural, technological, and scholarly systems that claim universality embed particular epistemological and political conditions that become visible when analysed from Global South subject positions.

I hold a PhD in English from Aligarh Muslim University (2025), and was awarded the Maulana Azad National Fellowship as Senior Research Fellow. I am the Founder of SHABD India.

Research
I investigate how cultural, technological, and scholarly systems that present themselves as universal produce different effects when encountered from postcolonial subject positions, using frameworks drawn from spatial theory, infrastructure studies, hermeneutic philosophy, and postcolonial criticism.

My current work centres on three connected enquiries. The first concerns how AI reading environments restructure the hermeneutic architecture of interpretation — introducing a computational intermediary into what the Western tradition has theorised as a dyadic encounter between reader and text, and what this means for the politics of knowledge and meaning-making. The second asks how digital simulation infrastructures (such as volunteer-run flight simulation networks) reproduce postcolonial hierarchies through what I term infrastructural inheritances: the persistence of historical standards, route imaginaries, and governance logics within contemporary digital systems. The third examines how globally circulating fields such as Digital Humanities and Electronic Literature land differently in Indian institutional soil — and what distinctively Indian frameworks might look like.

Across these projects, the consistent intellectual operation is to take a system, form, or framework that presents itself as culturally neutral, and demonstrate through close analysis that it is spatially, politically, and culturally situated, with particular attention to how it functions differently in Indian and Global South contexts.

I serve as Corresponding Guest Editor of a Scopus-indexed special issue in the Journal of Underrepresented and Minority Progress on "Digital Marginalisation, AI Bias, and Cultural Representation in South Asia" (forthcoming January 2027, co-edited with M. Rizwan Khan and Samya Brata Roy).

Algorithmic Hermeneutics
The process by which computational systems structure the interpretive pathways through which readers encounter textual meaning.
Infrastructural Inheritances
The persistence of historical standards, route imaginaries, and governance logics within contemporary digital systems.
Interpretive Infrastructure
AI reading environments functioning as infrastructure whose shaping of interpretation remains largely invisible, operating through a "double concealment."
The Fourth Epoch of Reading
AI-mediated reading as a historically distinct mode, distinguished from digital reading by the computational mediation of interpretation itself.
Techno-Indigeneity
Developing globally relevant yet locally grounded digital methodologies rooted in Indian epistemic traditions and material conditions.
Indian Postcolonial DH (IPDH)
A framework for Digital Humanities practice rooted in Indian postcolonial conditions, resisting the unreflective transplantation of Euro-American DH.
